## 2. OpenAI CLIP: Theoretical Foundations & Cosine Similarity

### A. The Vision-Language Latent Space
OpenAI’s Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP, architecture `ViT-B/32`) consists of two parallel neural encoders:
1. **Vision Transformer ($E_{\text{img}}$):** Ingests an image $I$, decomposes it into non-overlapping $32 \times 32$ pixel patches, and maps them to a continuous vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$.
2. **Text Transformer ($E_{\text{txt}}$):** Ingests a candidate textual description $T$, tokenizes it, processes it through self-attention layers, and maps it to a continuous vector $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$.

Both representations are projected onto the surface of a unit hypersphere through Euclidean $L_2$ normalization:
$$\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{u}} = \frac{\mathbf{u}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2}$$

### B. Cosine Similarity Formulation
The relative affinity between the visual evidence and the textual hypothesis is calculated as the inner dot product:
$$\text{Sim}(I, T) = \hat{\mathbf{v}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}^\top = \cos(\theta)$$
where $\theta$ is the angle between the two 512-dimensional vectors in latent space.

### C. Why Cosine Similarity is NOT a Probability
A common misconception among beginner analysts is equating cosine similarity with statistical probability:

| Property | Cosine Similarity $\cos(\theta)$ | Calibrated Probability $P(E \mid I)$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Output Range** | Real interval $[-1.0, 1.0]$ (typically $[0.15, 0.40]$ for zero-shot images) | Standard probability measure $[0.0, 1.0]$ |
| **Sum Constraint** | Independent dot products; does **not** sum to 1.0 across descriptions | $\sum_{i} P(E_i \mid I) = 1.0$ (Mutually exclusive hypothesis space) |
| **Calibration** | Uncalibrated geometric metric; higher score indicates higher semantic correlation, not likelihood | Calibrated frequentist or Bayesian measure of occurrence |
| **Prompt Sensitivity** | Slight phrasing shifts (*"a handgun"* vs. *"a firearm"*) yield different baseline scores | Invariant to lexical synonyms under ideal conditions |

## 3. Visual Counterfactual Intelligence (VCI)

### A. The Problem: Spurious Visual Correlations in Deep Networks
Deep vision models frequently rely on "shortcut learning" or contextual confounders. For example, in surveillance footage of a dark alleyway, a model might predict *"a robbery suspect"* based primarily on the asphalt road, night shadows, or brick wall texture rather than the physical individual present.

### B. Spatial Counterfactual Occlusion Mechanism
Visual Counterfactual Intelligence provides explainability through systematic perturbation:
1. **Baseline Evaluation ($S_0$):** Evaluate the unaltered evidence image $I$ against the target hypothesis $T$:
   $$S_0 = \text{Sim}(I, T)$$
2. **Spatial Grid Discretization ($N \times N$):** Discretize the image into $N \times N$ rectangular spatial patches $P_{r,c}$ for $r, c \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$.
3. **Neutral Patch Infilling:** Generate a perturbed image $I_{r,c}^{\text{masked}}$ where patch $P_{r,c}$ is replaced by an uninformative neutral grey rectangle with RGB values `(128, 128, 128)`.
4. **Counterfactual Score ($S_{r,c}$):** Re-evaluate the perturbed image:
   $$S_{r,c} = \text{Sim}(I_{r,c}^{\text{masked}}, T)$$
5. **Sensitivity Delta ($\Delta_{r,c}$):** Compute the difference:
   $$\Delta_{r,c} = S_0 - S_{r,c}$$

### C. Interpretation Matrix
- **$\Delta_{r,c} > 0$ (Decisive Evidence Patch):** When patch $(r, c)$ was masked, model similarity **dropped**. This indicates that the visual features within this patch directly supported the model's hypothesis.
- **$\Delta_{r,c} < 0$ (Contradictory / Suppressive Patch):** When patch $(r, c)$ was masked, model similarity **increased**. This indicates that this patch contained features that contradicted or suppressed the hypothesis.
- **$\Delta_{r,c} \approx 0$ (Neutral Background):** Masking this patch produced negligible impact on the model's evaluation.

### D. Sensitivity vs. Physical Causation
> [!IMPORTANT]
> VCI demonstrates **feature reliance**, not **physical necessity**.  
> If masking a region containing a suspect's jacket reduces confidence that the person has a weapon, VCI reveals that the model relied on the jacket context. It does not prove that the jacket caused the weapon to exist.

## 4. Surveillance Video Sampling & Temporal Aliasing

### A. Discrete Frame Extraction
Surveillance videos contain large numbers of redundant frames (e.g. 1 hour at 30 FPS = 108,000 frames). CrimeScene extracts frames at configurable temporal intervals:
$$t_k = k \times \Delta t, \quad k \in \{0, 1, \dots, M-1\}$$

### B. The Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Analogy & Micro-Events
When sampling video at discrete intervals (e.g., $\Delta t = 2.0\text{ seconds}$), any forensic event that begins and terminates entirely within a $<2\text{ second}$ window (e.g. rapid concealment of evidence or a brief flash) will not be captured in the sampled frames.

**Operational Best Practice:**
- **Initial Triage:** Use $\Delta t = 2.0\text{s}$ to $5.0\text{s}$ to scan broad footage.
- **Incident Investigation:** Once an event window is identified, re-run with $\Delta t = 0.5\text{s}$ or inspect all raw video frames in the relevant time slice.

## 5. Compliance with Forensic Evidence Standards

In jurisdictions adhering to the **Daubert Standard** (United States) or equivalent scientific evidence guidelines:
1. **Known Error Rates:** Multimodal zero-shot models have non-zero false positive rates depending on lighting, resolution, and lexical prompt engineering.
2. **Peer-Reviewed Scientific Foundations:** CLIP and counterfactual occlusion methods are established in computer vision literature (Radford et al., 2021; Zeiler & Fergus, 2014).
3. **Chain of Custody (ISO/IEC 27037):** CrimeScene processes uploaded video streams in read-only mode, writing temporary working copies without modifying original hash signatures of source media.
